🍄 Cordyceps

📰 Why in News?

Cordyceps, popularly known as the “caterpillar fungus”, has gained attention due to its increasing medicinal demand, ecological significance, and concerns over overharvesting in the Himalayan region.

🍄 About Cordyceps

  • A genus of parasitic fungi that mainly infect insects and arthropods.
  • Commonly called caterpillar fungus.
  • Scientifically important species: Ophiocordyceps sinensis.
  • Found mainly in the Himalayan alpine regions.
  • Occurs at elevations above 3000–5000 metres.

🐛 Unique Life Cycle

  • Fungal spores infect caterpillar larvae living underground.
  • The fungus gradually consumes the host body.
  • A fruiting fungal stalk emerges from the dead caterpillar.
  • Acts as a classic example of a parasite-host relationship.

🌿 Medicinal Importance

  • Widely used in traditional Tibetan and Chinese medicine.
  • Believed to improve:
    • Immunity
    • Stamina
    • Respiratory health
  • Contains bioactive compounds with pharmaceutical value.
  • High commercial demand makes it one of the world’s costliest biological resources.

⚠️ Threats & Concerns

  • Overharvesting due to rising market demand.
  • Climate change affecting alpine ecosystems.
  • Habitat degradation in Himalayan grasslands.
  • Declining natural populations in some regions.

🌍 Ecological Significance

  • Important component of fragile alpine ecosystems.
  • Indicates environmental health of Himalayan habitats.
  • Supports livelihood of mountain communities through seasonal collection.

🇮🇳 Distribution in India

  • Found in:
    • Uttarakhand
    • Himachal Pradesh
    • Sikkim
    • Arunachal Pradesh
  • Occurs in cold, high-altitude meadows.

📝 UPSC Prelims Key Points

  • Cordyceps is a parasitic fungus.
  • Associated with Himalayan alpine ecosystems.
  • Known as the caterpillar fungus.
  • Used in traditional medicine systems.
  • Important under Species in News & Biodiversity.

🦀 Indopotamon alipurduarense

Scientists have discovered a new species of freshwater crab named Indopotamon alipurduarense from the sub-Himalayan Dooars region of West Bengal.

🦀 About Indopotamon alipurduarense

  • Belongs to the genus Indopotamon.
  • Only the second known species under this genus.
  • Found in Alipurduar district of West Bengal.
  • Occurs in the Dooars landscape of the eastern Himalayas.
  • Lives inside deep burrows in muddy agricultural fields.
  • Mainly active during the monsoon season.

🎨 Physical Characteristics

  • Greyish-brown carapace (shell).
  • Distinctive bright orange-red claws.
  • Adapted to semi-terrestrial habitat conditions.
  • Strong burrowing ability helps survive seasonal changes.

🌍 Ecological Importance

  • Freshwater crabs help maintain wetland ecosystem health.
  • Play a role in nutrient recycling and soil aeration.
  • Serve as indicators of healthy freshwater habitats.
  • Important component of aquatic food chains.

⚠️ Threats

  • Habitat destruction due to agricultural expansion.
  • Wetland degradation and pollution.
  • Climate change affecting monsoon-dependent habitats.
  • Human disturbances in fragile ecosystems.

📍 About the Dooars Region

  • Located in the foothills of the eastern Himalayas.
  • Known for rich biodiversity and forest ecosystems.
  • Contains grasslands, wetlands, and riverine forests.
  • Important habitat for elephants, rhinos, and endemic fauna.

📝 UPSC Prelims Key Points

Methane Alert and Response System (MARS)

📰 Why in News?

The UNEP’s International Methane Emissions Observatory (IMEO) recently announced the expansion of the Methane Alert and Response System (MARS) to include emissions from coal mines and waste facilities.

🌍 About MARS

  • A global data-to-action platform launched under the UNEP.
  • Operates as part of the International Methane Emissions Observatory (IMEO).
  • First public global satellite-based system for detecting major methane emissions.
  • Announced during COP27 (Sharm el-Sheikh, 2022).
  • Began pilot operations in January 2023.

🛰️ Objectives

  • Detect large methane emission sources using satellites.
  • Notify governments and companies about detected emissions.
  • Support rapid mitigation and emission reduction actions.
  • Help achieve targets under the Paris Agreement and Global Methane Pledge.

⚙️ Working Mechanism

  • Satellite monitoring identifies methane “super-emitters”.
  • Alerts are sent to relevant stakeholders.
  • Countries and companies respond to reduce emissions.
  • Progress tracking ensures accountability and future prevention.

🌱 About Methane

  • Methane is a potent greenhouse gas.
  • Has significantly higher warming potential than carbon dioxide over short periods.
  • Major sources:
    • Fossil fuel extraction
    • Agriculture and livestock
    • Landfills and waste
    • Coal mining

🌎 Significance

  • Improves transparency in global methane monitoring.
  • Supports climate change mitigation efforts.
  • Encourages international cooperation on emission reduction.
  • Helps countries meet climate commitments.

📝 UPSC Prelims Key Points

  • MARS is linked with UNEP and IMEO.
  • Uses satellite technology for methane detection.
  • Launched during COP27.
  • Supports the Global Methane Pledge.
  • Important under Climate Change & Environment.

Caracal

📰 Why in News?

The Caracal was recently in news due to renewed discussions on conservation efforts and habitat protection for this rare wild cat species in India.

🐆 About Caracal

  • A medium-sized wild cat scientifically known as Caracal caracal.
  • Name derived from the Turkish word “Karakulak” meaning “black ears”.
  • Known for distinctive long black ear tufts.
  • Highly agile predator capable of leaping into the air to catch birds.

🌍 Distribution

  • Found in parts of Africa, the Middle East, and South Asia.
  • In India, mainly recorded from:
    • Rajasthan
    • Gujarat
    • Madhya Pradesh
  • Prefers:
    • Dry scrub forests
    • Semi-deserts
    • Grasslands

🌱 Conservation Status

  • IUCN Status: Least Concern (globally).
  • Population in India is considered highly threatened and declining.
  • Protected under Schedule I of the Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972.
  • Included in CITES Appendix I.

⚠️ Threats

  • Habitat loss and fragmentation.
  • Decline in prey base.
  • Human encroachment and agricultural expansion.
  • Illegal hunting and poaching.

🌿 Significance

  • Important predator in arid and semi-arid ecosystems.
  • Helps maintain ecological balance by controlling prey populations.
  • Indicator species for healthy grassland ecosystems.

📝 UPSC Prelims Key Points

  • Known for its distinctive black ear tufts.
  • Found in dry and semi-arid regions.
  • Protected under Schedule I of WPA, 1972.
  • Associated with grassland ecosystem conservation.
  • Important species in biodiversity and wildlife conservation current affairs.

    • Affects kidneys.
    • Can cause internal bleeding and kidney failure. :contentReference[oaicite:3]{index=3}

Peacock Tarantula (Gooty Tarantula)

📰 Why in News?

A scientific survey has been initiated to assess the population of Peacock Tarantula in the Nagarjunasagar-Srisailam Tiger Reserve.

🕷️ About Peacock Tarantula

  • Scientific name: Poecilotheria metallica.
  • Also known as Gooty Sapphire Ornamental Tarantula.
  • Belongs to family Theraphosidae (tarantulas).
  • One of the rarest and most visually striking spiders in the world.

🌍 Distribution & Habitat

  • Endemic to India – mainly found in Andhra Pradesh.
  • Occurs in limited forest patches of the Eastern Ghats.

🎨 Key Features

  • Distinctive metallic blue coloration with yellow and white markings.
  • Large size with 6–8 inches leg span.
  • Coloration due to structural reflection of light, not pigments.

⚙️ Behaviour & Biology

  • Nocturnal predator – active at night.
  • Does not rely on webs; uses speed and venom to capture prey.
  • Feeds on insects and small animals.
  • Females live up to 15 years; males have shorter lifespan.

⚠️ Conservation Status

  • IUCN Status: Critically Endangered.
  • Threats: Habitat loss, deforestation, and illegal pet trade.

🌱 Ecological Role

  • Helps in controlling insect populations.

Ru-Soam Bridges (Indigenous Engineering of Lepcha Community)

📰 Why in News?

UNESCO has partnered with the Sikkim government to document the indigenous engineering practice of Ru-Soam bridges in the Khangchendzonga Biosphere Reserve.

🌉 About Ru-Soam Bridges

  • Traditional cane bridges built by the Lepcha community in Sikkim.
  • Primarily found in the Khangchendzonga Biosphere Reserve.
  • In Lepcha language: ‘Ru’ = cane (rattan), ‘Soam’ = bridge.
  • Made entirely from locally available materials like bamboo, cane, and wood.

🔧 Structural Features

  • Main support: Two parallel canes called soamgyang, tied to trees at both ends.
  • Ahool: Swinging cane loops connecting the structure.
  • Soamgur: Bamboo used for the bridge deck.
  • Can span up to 100 metres; supports 2–3 persons at a time.

🌱 Ecological & Engineering Significance

  • Reflects sustainable and eco-friendly construction using renewable materials.
  • Demonstrates low carbon footprint compared to steel/concrete structures.
  • Highly flexible and resilient—suitable for seismic and flood-prone regions.
  • Example of indigenous knowledge systems and ecological harmony.

🌍 Scientific & Practical Importance

  • Useful for climate adaptation and disaster-resilient infrastructure planning.
  • Study aims to integrate traditional knowledge with modern engineering.
  • Showcases community-based sustainable development.

📝 UPSC Prelims Key Points

  • Associated with Lepcha tribe of Sikkim.
  • Located in Khangchendzonga Biosphere Reserve.
  • Made of bamboo, cane, and wood.
  • Example of indigenous eco-friendly engineering.
  • Important for climate resilience & sustainability.

Platypus (Ornithorhynchus anatinus)

📰 Why in News?

  • Recent fossil discoveries in South Australia revealed a new ancient platypus species with teeth, unlike modern platypuses.

📌 About Platypus

  • A semi-aquatic, egg-laying mammal endemic to eastern Australia.
  • Belongs to Monotremes (primitive mammals that lay eggs).
  • Scientific Name: Ornithorhynchus anatinus
  • One of the few venomous mammals (male has venomous spur).

🌍 Habitat

  • Found in freshwater ecosystems such as rivers, streams, creeks, and lakes.
  • Distribution: From tropical Queensland to Tasmania & Australian Alps.
  • Lives in burrows along riverbanks.

🧬 Key Features

  • Duck-like bill, beaver-like tail, and webbed feet.
  • Dense waterproof fur for thermal insulation.
  • Electroreceptors in bill help detect prey underwater.
  • Eyes, ears, nostrils close while diving.
  • Carnivorous – feeds on aquatic invertebrates.
  • No nipples – milk secreted through skin.

🛑 Conservation Status

  • IUCN Status: Near Threatened

⚠️ Threats

  • Habitat loss due to dams, urbanisation, and land clearing.
  • Water pollution and reduced river flows.
  • Climate change & droughts.
  • Accidental capture in fishing nets.

🌱 Conservation Efforts

  • Research programs on breeding and genetic conservation.
  • Reintroduction projects (e.g., NSW translocation).
  • Monitoring using environmental DNA (eDNA).
  • Protection of freshwater habitats.

📊 Key Facts for Prelims

  • Only mammal with electroreception.
  • Among the few venomous mammals.
  • One of the five living monotreme species.

Theridion himalayana (Himalayan Happy-Face Spider)

📰 Why in News?

Scientists have recently discovered a new species of spider named Theridion himalayana in the Western Himalayas of India.

🕷️ About Theridion himalayana

  • A newly discovered species of “happy-face spider” belonging to the family Theridiidae.
  • Discovered in the Western Himalayas (Uttarakhand region).
  • Named after the Himalayan region where it was first found.

🔍 Key Features

  • Polymorphic species – shows multiple forms in both sexes.
  • Exhibits about 32 different morphs with patterns resembling a smiling face.
  • Body patterns include combinations of red, black, and white markings.
  • Small-sized spider with distinct genital structures used for species identification.

⚙️ Unique Biological Traits

  • Copulatory ducts: Long, curved, hook-like structures.
  • Fertilisation ducts: Short, thin, arranged in a V-shape.
  • These reproductive features distinguish it from related species.

🌱 Ecological Role

  • Preys on small flies and insects, helping in natural pest control.
  • Plays a role in maintaining forest ecosystem balance.
  • May contribute to pollination (under study).

🌍 Scientific Significance

  • Shows parallel evolution similar to the Hawaiian happy-face spider (Theridion grallator).
  • Represents unique evolutionary lineage in Asian montane ecosystems.
  • Adds to India’s rich spider diversity (~1900+ species).

📝 UPSC Prelims Key Points

  • New species discovered in Western Himalayas (India).
  • Belongs to Theridiidae family (cobweb spiders).
  • Known as “happy-face spider” due to body patterns.
  • Polymorphism – multiple morphs (32 forms).
  • Important for insect control & ecosystem balance.

Atacama Desert

📰 Why in News?

The Atacama Desert has been in news due to concerns over light pollution affecting astronomical observations and increasing human activities in the region.

📍 Location

  • Located in northern Chile along the Pacific coast.
  • Situated between the Andes Mountains (east) and the Pacific Ocean (west).
  • Extends about 1,000–1,600 km along the coast.
  • Touches parts of Peru, Bolivia, and Argentina.

🌵 Key Features

  • World’s driest non-polar desert.
  • Average rainfall: about 1 mm/year; some areas receive no rainfall for decades.
  • Moderate temperatures (~18°C average).
  • Contains salt flats (salares), volcanoes, and dry riverbeds.
  • Rich in minerals like sodium nitrate, copper, lithium.

🌧️ Reasons for Extreme Aridity

  • Rain shadow effect of Andes and Coastal Range (double barrier).
  • Cold Humboldt Current reduces evaporation and cloud formation.
  • Presence of subtropical high-pressure system.

🔭 Importance

  • One of the best places in the world for astronomical observation due to clear skies.
  • Home to major observatories like ALMA (Atacama Large Millimeter Array).
  • Used as a Mars analogue for scientific research.

🌸 Unique Phenomenon

  • Experiences rare “Desert Bloom” after occasional rainfall.
  • Seeds remain dormant for years and bloom suddenly when moisture is available.

⚠️ Issues

  • Threat of light pollution from development projects affecting observatories.
  • Mining activities and waste dumping impacting fragile ecosystem.
  • Climate variability leading to unusual events (rare rainfall/snow).

📝 UPSC Prelims Key Points

  • Driest desert in the world (non-polar).
  • Located between Andes & Pacific Ocean.
  • Example of rain shadow desert.
  • Famous for astronomical observatories.
  • Known for desert bloom phenomenon.

Conservation Efforts:

Cyborg Botany

Context: Cyborg Botany is emerging as a novel interdisciplinary field that integrates plants with electronic systems to enable advanced environmental sensing and monitoring.

About Cyborg Botany:

  • It combines living plants with electronic components to create hybrid biological systems.
  • The term “cyborg” refers to a cybernetic organism.
  • It lies at the intersection of plant science, nanotechnology, and bioelectronics.

How It Works:

  • Nanowires and sensors are inserted into plant tissues to detect internal biochemical changes.
  • Conductive polymers (like PEDOT) act as pathways for electrical signal transmission.
  • Plants function as real-time environmental sensors.

Applications:

  • Environmental Monitoring: Detect pollutants, soil moisture, and ecosystem changes.
  • Smart Agriculture: Enables precision farming and crop health tracking.
  • Climate Research: Helps in studying plant responses to climate change.

Challenges:

  • Ethical concerns regarding modification of living organisms.
  • Technical difficulty in integrating electronics with plant systems.
  • High cost and limited scalability.

UPSC Relevance:

  • Prelims: Science & Technology – Emerging technologies.
  • Mains GS III: Role of technology in environmental conservation and agriculture.

Lake Issyk-Kul

Context: Recently, underwater archaeologists discovered a hidden ancient city beneath Lake Issyk-Kul, indicating a lost civilisation dating back around 700 years.

About Lake Issyk-Kul:

  • Located in northeastern Kyrgyzstan.
  • Surrounded by the Tian Shan Mountains (“Heaven’s Mountains”).
  • It is the world’s second-largest high mountain lake after Lake Titicaca.

Key Features:

  • Formed around 25 million years ago and has an endorheic basin (no outflow).
  • Water is brackish (slightly saline).
  • Altitude: ~1600 m.
  • Area: ~6,280 sq km; Length: 182 km; Width: up to 61 km.
  • Maximum depth: 668 m (one of the deepest lakes in the world).
  • Second-largest saline lake after the Caspian Sea.

Unique Characteristics:

  • Does not freeze in winter due to depth, low salinity, and warm springs.
  • Name “Issyk-Kul” means “Hot Lake”.
  • Shows diverse landscapes from subtropical to tundra around its shores.

Conservation Status:

  • Designated as a Ramsar Site (1976) – wetland of international importance.

UPSC Relevance:

  • Prelims: Important lakes, endorheic basins, Ramsar sites.
  • Mains GS I: Physical geography (lakes, mountains).

Olive Ridley turtles were recently in news due to large-scale nesting and conservation efforts along India’s eastern coastline.

About Olive Ridley Turtles:

  • Scientific name: Lepidochelys olivacea.
  • They are the most abundant sea turtle species in the world.
  • Named after their olive-green coloured carapace.

Habitat & Distribution:

  • Found in tropical and subtropical waters of the Pacific, Indian, and Atlantic Oceans.

Unique Feature:

  • Known for mass nesting called “Arribada”, where thousands of turtles come ashore simultaneously.

  • Marine pollution and climate change.

Conservation Efforts:

  • Operation Olivia by Indian Coast Guard to protect nesting sites.
  • Use of Turtle Excluder Devices (TEDs) in fishing nets.
  • Community participation and beach protection measures.

Wheat Crop – Climatic Conditions

Why in News?
Rising temperatures and changing climatic conditions are affecting wheat production in India, raising concerns over food security.

Climatic Conditions Required for Wheat:

  • Wheat is a temperate crop grown in cool seasons.
  • Requires cool and moist climate during growth and warm, dry climate at maturity.

Temperature:

  • Optimum temperature for growth: 21°C to 24°C.
  • Can tolerate range: 4°C to 35°C.
  • High temperature during flowering leads to reduced yield.

Rainfall:

  • Requires about 30–38 cm rainfall during growing season.
  • Excess rainfall can lead to diseases and crop damage.

Sunlight:

  • Needs bright sunshine during grain ripening.

Soil Requirements:

  • Best suited to well-drained loamy soil.
  • Requires good availability of nitrogen, phosphorus, and potassium.

Impact of Climate Change:

  • Rising temperature causes early maturity and reduced grain filling.
  • Heat stress during flowering significantly lowers productivity.
  • Erratic rainfall affects sowing and yield stability.

Leaf Spot Diseases

Why in News?
Leaf spot disease in arecanut plantations has emerged as a major concern, and government institutes in Karnataka are conducting field demonstrations to guide farmers on effective management practices.

About Leaf Spot Diseases:

  • Leaf spot diseases refer to a group of plant diseases caused by fungi, bacteria, or other microorganisms.
  • These pathogens infect the leaf surface and produce visible spots or lesions.
  • The spots can vary in size, shape, and color and may coalesce in severe cases.
  • They interfere with photosynthesis, affecting plant growth and productivity.

Causes:

  • Majorly caused by fungi (about 85%) and also by bacteria.
  • Environmental factors like:
    • Water stress
    • Sunscald
    • Chemical injury (pesticides/herbicides)
    • Nutrient imbalance
    can produce similar symptoms.

Symptoms:

  • Appearance of small to large spots or lesions on leaves.
  • Leaves may turn yellow or brown.
  • Severe infection leads to shriveling and premature leaf drop.

Distribution & Occurrence:

  • Occurs in almost all regions of the world.
  • More prevalent in humid and wet climates.
  • Affects:
    • Vegetables
    • Fruit trees
    • Ornamental plants
    • Shrubs

Impact:

  • Leads to reduced crop productivity.
  • Poses a threat to food security and economic stability.
  • Can severely damage plant foliage if untreated.

Control & Prevention:

  • Use of cultural practices (crop rotation, sanitation).
  • Biological control methods.
  • Judicious use of chemical fungicides.
  • Integrated disease management is most effective.

About Sea Slugs:

  • Sea slugs are soft-bodied marine molluscs found across all oceans, from shallow waters to the deep sea.

  • They resemble snails but lack external shells (naked snails).

  • Commonly found in coral reef ecosystems.

  • Feed on algae, sponges, and small invertebrates.

  • Known for bright colours and patterns (warning signals to predators).

  • Have sensory tentacles to detect chemicals and movement.
  • Some species can photosynthesize using sunlight.
  • Capable of regeneration of body parts.
  • Few species can even incorporate genes from prey.

Behaviour:

  • Most are diurnal, though some species are nocturnal.

Forest Soils Identified as Hidden Methane Sinks: New Study

Context: Scientists have discovered that forest soils actively contribute to climate mitigation by absorbing methane (CH₄), a highly potent greenhouse gas.

Key Discovery:

  • Forest soils are not passive but actively remove greenhouse gases.
  • They absorb methane (CH₄), which is significantly more potent than CO₂.
  • This methane absorption capacity is increasing under certain climate conditions.

Scientific Findings:

  • Based on a long-term study (up to 24 years) across forest sites in Germany.
  • Under warmer and drier conditions, soils show increased methane uptake.
  • Methane absorption increased by approximately 3% per year.

Why Methane Matters:

  • Methane (CH₄) is about 25–28 times more potent than CO₂ in the short term.
  • Even small reductions in methane significantly impact climate change mitigation.

Role of Forest Soils:

  • Forest soils store up to ~70% of total forest carbon.
  • Now also confirmed as:
    • Carbon sink
    • Methane sink

Mechanism:

  • Special microbes called methanotrophs consume methane.
  • Oxygen-rich soils enhance methane absorption.
  • Warm and dry conditions boost microbial activity.

Limitations:

  • Benefit occurs only under specific environmental conditions.
  • Excess moisture reduces methane uptake.
  • Extreme climate change may still damage forest ecosystems.

Threats:

  • Deforestation and logging
  • Soil disturbance due to land-use change
  • Altered rainfall patterns

Significance:

  • Suggests climate models may underestimate forests’ role.
  • Highlights importance of soil conservation.
  • Helps improve climate predictions and forest management strategies.

Policy Implications:

  • Protect undisturbed forest ecosystems
  • Minimize soil disturbance
  • Promote natural forests over monocultures
  • Increase investment in soil research

Conclusion: Forest soils act as a hidden climate regulator by functioning as both carbon and methane sinks, strengthening Earth’s natural climate defense system.
